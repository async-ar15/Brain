# Theory - Final Interview Simulation

This is it. The culmination of 30 days of intense Data Structures and Algorithms practice.

## The Interview Arch

A typical 45-minute technical interview looks like this:

- **0:00 - 0:05:** Introductions. (Have a 30-second elevator pitch ready about who you are and what you've been working on).
- **0:05 - 0:10:** The interviewer pastes the problem. You read it, ask clarifying questions, and establish constraints.
- **0:10 - 0:15:** You brainstorm and agree on an optimal approach.
- **0:15 - 0:35:** You write the code, talking through your logic.
- **0:35 - 0:40:** You dry-run the code, fix any bugs, and state final time/space complexities.
- **0:40 - 0:45:** "Do you have any questions for me?" (Always have 2-3 thoughtful questions prepared about the company's tech stack, culture, or the interviewer's day-to-day).

## What if I bomb the interview?

Every software engineer, including Staff and Principal engineers at FAANG, has bombed interviews. It is a rite of passage.

An interview evaluates your performance on one specific problem on one specific day. It is a highly noisy signal. It does not define your worth as an engineer.

If you fail, write down the problem immediately after the interview ends. Figure out the optimal solution later that day. Add it to your study list. Then, move on to the next one.

**You are ready. Good luck.**
